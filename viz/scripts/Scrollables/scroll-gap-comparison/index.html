01 / The spread

Every line is a major.

Each horizontal line shows the five-year gap for one major. The zero line is the point where student and faculty medians meet.

02 / Convergence

The lines come together.

The major-level gaps collapse toward one shared position. The color remains so the individual cases do not disappear into the summary.

03 / The average

One average, many majors.

The converged marks become the average of the valid major-level median gaps. It is a summary of gaps, not an average salary.

04 / Pull apart

The average hides the range.

The full set of major gaps returns, making the distance between the extremes visible again.

05 / The extremes

Largest versus smallest.

The final bars isolate the single largest and smallest five-year gaps side by side.

06 / Unpacking the extremes

Where the distance comes from.

The same largest- and smallest-gap majors become vertical ranges: student five-year median earnings at one endpoint, faculty total compensation at the other.

How to read it. The gap is faculty median total compensation (`totalpaybenefits`) minus student median earnings five years after entry (`EARN_MDN_5YR`). The average is the mean of valid major-level median gaps; the populations and time scales are different.
